Cost optimisation guide

Practical levers for cutting the cost of a session, followed by the mechanisms — context usage, caching, prices, billing modes — that produce it.

1Before starting

  • Is the open session still of use to the next request? If not, /clear.
  • Is the place to act on already known? Naming it is worth more than any other optimisation.
  • Is the task mechanical, ordinary or hard? Choose the model accordingly.
  • Are there MCP servers running that the current project does not use?

2While working

  • Long logs or dumps: name the file, do not paste its contents.
  • Broad searches through the code: delegate them to a sub-agent.
  • Two failed attempts on the same point: stop, start again clean, move up a model if needed.
  • Verification: run the test instead of asking the model to confirm.

3Every so often

  • Review CLAUDE.md: does it hold only what is needed almost always?
  • Check the ratio of fresh tokens to total tokens on the dashboard.
  • Watch the average fresh tokens per interaction: if it grows, sessions are running too long.
  • Check that the price list matches current prices.

FIRST THING TO DO

Close the session with /clear when the subject changes. The cost of a session grows with the square of its length (explained in «How the cost builds up» below): it is the single action with the greatest effect on the overall bill.

1
High impact

Close the session when the subject changes

A /clear between two unrelated activities brings the resent context back to almost nothing. Keeping alive a session that has been through five different subjects means paying for all five at every turn.

The criterion is the change of subject, not the time elapsed: if the next request needs nothing of what came before, there is no reason to carry it along. It is the lever with the greatest effect on the overall bill, because of the quadratic mechanism described in «How the cost builds up».

2
High impact

Point at the exact spot instead of making it search

«Fix the login bug» forces exploration: searches, whole files read, attempts, all of it resent at every following turn. auth.ts:42, the token expires before the refresh reaches the same result without loading anything superfluous.

It takes a few seconds more to phrase and can keep tens of thousands of tokens out of the permanent context, never resent for all the turns that follow.

3
High impact

Stop fruitless attempts by the second one

When the model repeats variations of the same fix without getting closer to the solution, every further turn costs more and succeeds less often, because the context fills up with failed attempts.

The right move: interrupt, start again from a clean context carrying what the failure taught, move up a model if warranted. It avoids the most expensive and least productive tail of spending.

4
Medium impact

Keep CLAUDE.md lean

Included at every turn of every session. Two thousand tokens of useful conventions pay for themselves at once; ten thousand tokens of discursive documentation are a permanent fixed tax multiplied by every turn of the whole team. Only what is needed almost always belongs there.

5
Medium impact

Turn off MCP servers that are not in use

Every MCP server puts its tools' schemas into the context at every turn, whether or not they get used. Keeping only those useful to the current project takes a one-off check and produces a steady saving over time.

6
Medium impact

Delegate exploration to a sub-agent

A sub-agent works in a context of its own and returns only the conclusion. It pays off when the search is broad and the answer is short; it does not on small tasks, where the cost of starting one exceeds the saving.

7
Medium impact

Name the files instead of pasting their contents

A stack trace pasted into the chat stays in the context until the end of the session. The same file left on disk, with a pointer to where to look, gets read selectively and costs a fraction — a single large paste can cost more than the entire session containing it.

8
Medium impact

Work in continuous blocks

The cache has a default TTL of five minutes. Three turns close together reuse the context at a tenth of the price; the same three turns spread across the morning pay for it almost in full every time — up to about __EX_RATIO__× on the input cost of a long session.

9
Low impact

Plan once and execute for a long stretch

On broad work, an initial planning pass produces an outline that execution follows without second thoughts, instead of ten changes of direction each resending the whole context. It pays off on large jobs; it is overhead on small ones.

10
Low impact

Have the machine verify, not the model

Asking for confirmation that something is correct costs a turn of reasoning and produces an opinion. Running the test costs almost nothing and produces a fact. Small but frequent: good practice regardless of cost.

A flat plan changes little

These priorities stay the same. A flat plan's allowance is weighted by model, so a pricier model eats into it at roughly the same rate as it would eat into a pay-as-you-go budget: it is not a free token. The one real difference is that if you never come close to the plan's limit, optimising is pointless — below the threshold, go ahead and use the best model with no further arithmetic. Near the limit, though, exactly the same levers above apply, with the unit of measure changed from euros to minutes of blocked work.

The eternal session

One session kept open for days across different subjects. It is the most expensive way to use the tool and it also degrades the quality of the answers.

The cheap model on principle

Always choosing the least expensive model to save money, and getting three failed attempts instead of one that works. You pay more, in money and in time.

Micro-saving at your own expense

Spending five minutes phrasing the shortest possible request to save a few cents. Your working time is worth orders of magnitude more.

The encyclopaedic CLAUDE.md

Extensive documentation placed where it is re-read at every turn. An invisible fixed cost multiplied by every developer and every session.

Compaction that just happens to you

Letting automatic compaction step in halfway through a line of reasoning instead of closing it or compacting at a natural boundary.

«It is flat anyway»

Assuming that with a subscription the pricier model is free. It eats the allowance at the same rate: the bill arrives as a block, not as an invoice.

How it works

The natural intuition is that you pay for what Claude writes. In an agentic coding session it is almost the opposite: the dominant cost is the context resent at every turn — system instructions, CLAUDE.md, tool schemas, files read, command output, every previous message.

One unintuitive consequence follows: the cost of a session does not grow in proportion to its length, but much faster. If every turn adds a more or less constant amount of context, the total resent after n turns grows like n². Doubling the length of a session quadruples its cost.

A WORKED EXAMPLE

A session adding about 3,000 tokens of context per turn, carried on for 40 turns, resends roughly 2.46 million tokens in total. The same 40 interactions split into 4 sessions of 10 turns resend 660,000: 3.7 times less, for exactly the same work done.

Prompt caching fixes part of the problem

Repeated context gets cached and reading it back costs a fraction. The multipliers apply to the input price and are the same for every model.

Token usage depends on the model

The token count is not independent of the model. A weaker model on a hard task does not simply use a little more: it takes wrong turns, re-reads files, tries corrections, and every attempt is one more turn resending all the context accumulated so far.

A cheap model's failure is paid for in working time

This item appears on no price list: when the cheapest model does not get to the end of the task, the difference is paid in developer time, not only in tokens. An hour of human work is worth, as an order of magnitude, as much as tens of hours of intensive Claude Code use.

Kind of taskModelWhy
Mechanical and specifiedHaiku Renames, reformatting, pulling data out of logs, repetitive edits with a clear rule. Usage is almost identical to the reference model's, so the saving is real.
Ordinary developmentSonnet The correct default for most of the work. It needs no justification.
Hard or high-riskOpus Non-obvious debugging, architectural refactoring, reasoning about unfamiliar code. It pays for itself as soon as it cuts the number of turns past the threshold in the table above.
Already failed onceMove up a model Insisting with the same model on a task it has already got wrong is the most reliable way to burn tokens with nothing to show.

SCENARIO A

Pay as you go

Pay-as-you-go API billing · per token

SCARCE RESOURCE
Money, continuously and proportionally.
MARGINAL COST OF A TOKEN
Constant and always positive. Every token used appears on the invoice.
SHAPE OF THE CURVE
Linear. No threshold and no ceiling: the tenth million tokens costs what the first did.
SIGNAL TO WATCH
Total spending, on the invoice and on the dashboard.

SCENARIO B

Flat subscription

Subscription plan · fixed monthly fee

SCARCE RESOURCE
The plan's usage allowance, which refills in windows.
MARGINAL COST OF A TOKEN
Zero up to the limit, then blocking. It is a step function, not a straight line.
SHAPE OF THE CURVE
Flat, then a wall. Past the threshold you do not pay more: you simply stop.
SIGNAL TO WATCH
How many times a month you end up blocked before the window refills.

IN SHORT

On a flat subscription an expensive model is not free. Plan allowances are not counted in messages: they are weighted by model, and a pricier model eats the allowance at roughly the same rate as it would eat a pay-as-you-go budget. So there is no token budget to save by paying more: they are not two separate currencies, they are the same currency with two labels.

The real asymmetry is a different one, and it is the step: if you never touch the plan's limit, optimising has no value at all. The token saved does not turn into money, does not accumulate and serves no purpose, while the time spent saving it is a dead loss. Below the threshold the right strategy is to use the best model and think no further.

The moment you start reaching the limit, though, the allowance goes back to behaving like money: from there on exactly the same levers as the pay-as-you-go scenario apply, with the unit of measure changed from euros to minutes of blocked work.

What changes in practice

AspectPay as you goFlat
Below the threshold There is no below-the-threshold: you always pay, so every waste removed is money saved right away. Optimising is counterproductive: it costs time and produces no benefit at all.
Choosing the expensive model Justified by the arithmetic in the previous section: fewer turns and a lower risk of failure. Justified the same way, but the currency is the allowance. It is not a neutral choice.
Exploratory attempts They have a price. Better to fence them off with a sub-agent or a dedicated session. With allowance to spare there is no reason to hold back: it is the best use of a resource that would otherwise expire unused.
The shared rule In both scenarios the principle is identical: cut the waste, never the use. A turn spent resending useless context is waste. A turn spent solving a real problem is not, and must not be cut in either case.
